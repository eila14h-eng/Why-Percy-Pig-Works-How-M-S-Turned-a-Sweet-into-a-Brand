# Why-Percy-Pig-Works-How-M-S-Turned-a-Sweet-into-a-Brand
A short brand case study on M&amp;S's Percy Pig, built as a slide deck using published sources.

The question

Percy Pig began in the early 1990s as a piggy-shaped gummy sweet. Why has he become one of M&S Food's most valuable assets?

What I found

The deck answers the question through three reasons:

	1.	Character. Percy works like a brand character, not just a product. He joined TikTok in 2020, starred in an M&S Christmas Food ad voiced by Tom Holland, and has his own plaque at the Paddington food hall.
	2.	Range. Around his 30th birthday there were 12 types of Percy sweets and over 50 Percy items on the M&S website, including bedding and baking mixes. M&S's stated plan is to explore new categories while protecting and growing the core products.
	3.	Loyalty. M&S's court filing cited a customer with a Percy tattoo, Swizzels agreed to redesign a similar sweet after M&S protected the brand's design, and all Percy sweets have been gelatine-free since May 2019.

The deck also looks at international growth (over 50,000 bags a week through Target in the US, and a launch in Coles supermarkets in Australia) and finishes with my own recommendations: protect the core, extend with discipline, and let data decide which launches add sales.

Why I chose Percy

I grew up with Percy Pig, and because pigs are my favourite animal, I've always felt a real connection to the brand that goes beyond the sweets. That connection makes me more likely to buy his other merchandise, not just the sweets themselves. I think this is a very good idea, because it turns a much-loved product into a character people want to collect and keep buying.

Limitations

	•	All figures are as reported in the sources below at the time, and some date from around Percy's 30th birthday, so they may be out of date.
	•	This is desk research from public sources. I have not used M&S sales data.
	•	The recommendations are my own opinion, not M&S's plans.
